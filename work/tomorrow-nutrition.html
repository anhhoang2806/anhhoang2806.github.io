Tomorrow’s Nutrition: 22,300 organic clicks on a supplements brand, and an average position that says the job is unfinished

Measured Dec 2023 – Apr 2025 · Freelance · Health & supplements · Organic search

A health supplements brand whose product name does more search work than its company name. This page reports what Google Search Console shows across about 16 months, and is straight about what it does not show.

01 — At a glance

What does the Tomorrow’s Nutrition organic search data show?

Across about 16 months, from 22 December 2023 to 21 April 2025, Tomorrow’s Nutrition recorded 22,300 organic clicks from 741,000 impressions, at an average CTR of 3% and an average position of 11.1. That is real volume for a small supplements brand. It is also a position that leaves most of those impressions unconverted, and this page treats that as the finding rather than hiding it under the click total.

02 — The account

What is the Tomorrow’s Nutrition account?

Tomorrow’s Nutrition is a health and supplements brand. Its flagship product brand is Sunfiber, and the site sells and explains that product.

This is organic search work. Everything on this page comes out of Google Search Console, which reports what people typed, what Google showed them, and what they clicked. It reports nothing about advertising, and there is no advertising in these figures.

03 — The query mix

What does the query mix say?

The six queries below are the top of the account by clicks. Four of them are the product brand in one spelling or another, and two of them are the company brand.

Sunfiber variants account for 7,066 clicks. Company-brand queries account for 1,453. The product brand outpulls the company brand roughly 5 to 1.

People search the product, not the company

That split is a strategy conclusion, not a curiosity. If the demand arrives under the product name, the product is what the site should be organised around: the strongest pages, the internal links and the structured data should all point at Sunfiber as the entity, with Tomorrow’s Nutrition described as the brand behind it rather than the other way round.

It also sets the ceiling honestly. Almost everything at the top of this account is somebody who already knows the name. Non-brand demand is the growth that has not been built yet.

04 — The headroom

Where is the headroom?

An average position of 11.1 across 741,000 impressions means the site sits mostly at the bottom of page one or the top of page two. Google is already showing these pages to people. It is showing them a rank or two below where the clicks are.

That is worth saying plainly: this is an unfinished job, not a finished one. The demand exists, the impressions are being served, and the site is not yet ranked well enough to take its share of them. The work that follows from that is ordinary and specific — improving the pages that already rank in the eight-to-fifteen band rather than chasing new terms, since a page at 11 has already proved Google considers it relevant.

One product, two spellings, two jobs

“sunfiber” as one word drew 3,902 clicks from 98,282 impressions. “sun fiber” as two words drew 1,899 clicks from 34,933 impressions. Same product, same intent, different spelling — and the two-word version is not a typo to be ignored. Across the six queries in the table above it carries over a fifth of the branded impressions.

The implication is that the spelling needs deciding rather than leaving to chance. One page owns the canonical spelling and carries the product entity; the two-word spelling is handled as an alternate name on that same page rather than by a second page competing with it. Two pages chasing one intent split the signal and neither wins.

05 — What changed

What changed, and what I cannot prove?

The click curve steps up from roughly November 2024. Before that point clicks track well below impressions; after it the two lines converge and both rise. The impressions were largely already there, so what moved is click-through and position on demand that already existed, not new demand.

I can see that step-change in the curve. I did not pull a period-over-period comparison, so I cannot put a percentage on it, and I am not going to. A dated before-and-after would turn this from a volume snapshot into a demonstrated result, and until that comparison is pulled the honest claim is the one above: the shape of the curve changed, by an amount this page does not state.

06 — What I would do differently

What would I do differently?

The weakness on this account is measurement, not strategy. I can describe what the account looks like across 16 months. I cannot show what it looked like before, so every claim on this page has to stay a description.

I would have pulled the period comparison at the time

The step-change from around November 2024 is visible in the curve, and that is all I have. Two fixed windows exported while the work was running would have cost minutes and would have settled the question for good. Because I did not do that, section 05 has to say the shape changed and refuse to say by how much, which is the weakest sentence on the page and an avoidable one.

I would have fixed one window and reported it once

There are two readings of this property in front of me: 22,300 clicks over 22 December 2023 to 21 April 2025, and 21,100 clicks over a shorter view ending about a month earlier. Same dataset, two cut-offs. That is how one result ends up being read as two, and the fix is to pick the window before pulling anything and state it beside every figure.

I would have reported brand and non-brand separately

All six of the top queries are brand: four spellings of the product, two of the company. A single clicks total on an account shaped like that mostly counts people who already knew the name. Split into two lines, brand and non-brand, the total stops flattering the work and starts saying whether anything is reaching people who have not heard of Sunfiber.
