02 — The situation

What was the situation at ELSA Speak?

ELSA Speak is an AI English-learning app. Installs are the business. Not sessions, not impressions, not keyword counts — installs, measured month over month, and then what those installs do in the funnel afterwards.

That reframes the whole job. Organic search matters only to the extent that it puts the app on a phone, and then only to the extent that the person opens it. So the work splits three ways: get the app found in the app stores, get the web traffic to convert into installs, and be able to prove which of the two did it.

I also picked up the funnel beyond the install. I work with the CRM teams on install-to-trial and trial-to-subscription rates, because an install that never becomes a subscription is not growth.

03 — The constraint

What was the constraint?

Five priority markets in five languages, on two app stores, with metadata rules that differ per store and per locale. ASO is not translation — the keyword a Japanese learner types is not the translated form of the keyword a US learner types, and the character limits are not the same either.

The harder constraint was underneath all of it: I could not trust the numbers I was being measured on. More on that below, because it turned out to be the most important thing I did here.

04 — What I did

What did I actually do?

ASO keyword research and metadata localization, per market

I ran keyword research and metadata localization across 8–10 markets rather than translating a single master listing. Each market got its own keyword set, its own title and subtitle structure, and its own iteration cycle against install volume.

Result over the first three months: installs up 84%.

In the US App Store, “english app” moved from position 2 to position 1. That is a head term in the US App Store, and position 1 versus position 2 is not a small difference in install volume.

ELSA Speak · installs, Mar–May 2026

Technical SEO for the global site

I own site architecture, performance and indexation for the global web presence, aligned specifically to organic and install growth rather than to traffic for its own sake. The site’s job is to hand the user to the app store with the attribution intact.

The estate is 70,075 indexed pages across eight sites — Global, VN, TH, TW, JP, HK, BR and ID. The indexation audit came back with 54,969 404s, 14,330 canonical errors, 3,020 schema errors, 2,878 redirects, 9 soft 404s and 1,020 duplicate pages to merge. About 80% of what it found is fixed.

Organic followed: Vietnam +30.6% from March to August 2026, and +56.8% measured off the May trough, with all markets +25.1% off that same trough. These are Ahrefs estimates rather than Search Console figures, so they are directional — the install number is the one measured end to end.

A semantic embedding and vector database over ~20,000 URLs

I built a vector database of around 20,000 Vietnamese content URLs. It powers four things:

  • semantic search across the corpus
  • deduplication, which at that scale is not something you can do by hand
  • topic clustering, to see where coverage is thin and where it is redundant
  • RAG retrieval

At twenty thousand URLs, “do we already have a page about this” stops being answerable with a spreadsheet. Embeddings make it a query.

Defending AI share-of-voice, and finding ChatGPT in the install data

Our presence in Google AI Overviews was declining. I tracked it in Ahrefs Brand Radar and worked to defend share-of-voice as that surface moved against us.

While looking at where installs were actually originating, I identified ChatGPT as a material new install source — not a rounding error, a real channel — across Japan, Vietnam, Thailand and Taiwan, and scaled it. That is a channel that did not exist in anyone’s attribution model a short time ago, and it only shows up if you are looking at source data rather than at a dashboard someone built two years ago.

05 — The attribution audit

Correction · not a growth figure

How did I find a 10x error in our own reported SEO installs?

This is the part of this role I would most want to be judged on.

ELSA reported a healthy volume of SEO-attributed app installs. Nothing was flagged. No tags were broken, no integration was down, and the dashboards were green.

The number was wrong by roughly an order of magnitude.

The cause was a methodology issue in how AppsFlyer was attributing the web-to-app journey — the way the attribution was being credited, not a failure of tracking. That distinction matters, because a tracking outage announces itself and a methodology error does not. It just quietly produces a plausible number, forever.

I traced it, proved it, and corrected the reported figure downward by roughly 10x.

I want to be precise about what that cost. It removed a large share of the organic installs my own team was credited with. Reporting the correction meant reporting that my channel was substantially smaller than the business believed.

I did it because the alternative is worse. An inflated organic number does not sit still — it gets used. Paid budget gets moved away from channels that are actually working. Headcount gets argued for on the strength of it. Roadmap priorities get set against it. Every one of those decisions is then wrong, and nobody can tell why, because the source number looks fine.

Which numbers this correction applies to

The 84% install lift in section 01 came from ASO — keyword research and metadata localization in the app stores, across 8–10 markets. The correction described here applies to web-to-app SEO-attributed installs reported through AppsFlyer. They are two different measurements, taken from two different systems, and this page keeps them apart deliberately. The growth figure is not the corrected figure, and the corrected figure is not a growth figure.

The second bug

The same audit surfaced something else. A domain-exclusion rule was silently blocking Smart Banners on regional subdomains. The banners were configured. They were simply never serving to a portion of regional traffic, and nothing reported an error.

I re-architected the setup to one AppsFlyer workspace per subdomain, which removed the exclusion conflict at the root rather than patching around it.

What this is evidence of

Two things, and they are the same thing. First, that I will go looking underneath a number that nobody has asked me to question. Second, that I will report what I find even when it makes my own results look smaller.

07 — What I would do differently

What would I do differently?

I would audit attribution in week one, before agreeing to any baseline. I found the 10x issue, but I found it after the reporting had already been running. Everything measured against that baseline before the correction was measured against a number that was wrong. I now treat an attribution audit as the first deliverable of any app growth role, before I accept a target — because a target set against an inflated baseline is a target nobody can hit honestly.

I would test Smart Banner delivery per subdomain rather than per configuration. The domain-exclusion bug survived because the setup looked correct. Checking that something is configured is not the same as checking that it is serving. I would add a per-subdomain delivery check to the standing monitoring rather than relying on a configuration review.

I would have started tracking non-Google AI sources earlier. ChatGPT turned out to be a material install source across four markets. It was material before I started measuring it — I just could not see it. The lesson is that the attribution model needs a route for sources that did not exist when the model was built, otherwise new channels arrive as unattributed traffic and get ignored.
